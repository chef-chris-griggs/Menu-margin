The best recipe costing option depends on how many dishes you manage, how often your supplier prices change and whether you need to save and reuse recipes. Compare using a dish from your own menu rather than relying on feature lists alone.

Option 1: Use a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet can suit a chef who costs only a few dishes or updates prices occasionally. It offers full control but requires you to maintain formulas, supplier prices and ingredient units yourself. Start with a reusable template and check your working carefully when ingredients or yields change.

Option 2: Use a free food costing calculator

A free single-recipe calculator suits quick portion-cost and GP checks before committing to a subscription. Mise en Price offers a public calculator without login or card. Its entries are not automatically saved to an account, so copy your figures if you need to keep them.

Option 3: Use dedicated recipe costing software

Dedicated software is more useful when you manage a recipe library and many supplier pack prices. Mise en Price offers recipe costing, saved recipes, a supplier price book, recipe photo/PDF scanning with manual review, invoice scanning with manual review and CSV export. It provides a 14-day card-free trial; the optional subscription is £9.99 per month thereafter. Check current features yourself with one of your regular dishes.

A checklist for comparing costing tools

Test how the software handles grams, millilitres and each; purchased pack size; real saleable yields; ingredient-based GP based on prices excluding VAT; supplier price changes; scanned text corrections; CSV exports; and the number of recipes you need to maintain. Check whether labour, overheads and waste are included. A tool that calculates only ingredient costs cannot report your true net profit.

What about other software suppliers?

Use the same real recipe and supplier prices across any competing product you consider. Check each supplier's current pricing and UK VAT handling directly before purchase. This guide deliberately avoids unverified rankings or claims about competitors. The word 'best' here describes the selection method, not an independently tested market leaderboard.

Try a real recipe before choosing

Cost a twenty-portion batch, add its realistic garnish and sauce costs, and compare the resulting price and GP against your current menu. Recheck the calculation after changing one supplier price. This will show whether you need a spreadsheet, a free calculator or a saved recipe system.
