How to calculate food gross profit (GP)

GP per portion = selling price excluding VAT − ingredient cost per portion. Food-cost GP percentage = (GP per portion ÷ selling price excluding VAT) × 100.

For example, a dish sold for £12.00 excluding VAT with £3.60 in ingredient costs has £8.40 ingredient-based GP per portion and a 70% GP.

Worked example

Selling price (excluding VAT)£12.00
Ingredient cost per portion£3.60
GP per portion£8.40
Food-cost GP percentage70%
Food cost percentage30%

Illustrative ingredient-based gross profit. Labour, energy, overheads and waste are not automatically deducted. Compare costs and selling prices on a consistent VAT basis.

How is GP different from food cost percentage?

Food cost percentage is ingredient cost divided by selling price. GP percentage is the remainder after ingredient cost. For example, at 30% food cost, ingredient-based GP is 70%.

How do I work backwards from a target GP?

To price a dish at a target ingredient-based GP, divide its ingredient cost per portion by 1 − target GP as a decimal. For a £3 ingredient cost and 75% target GP, the excluding-VAT selling price would be £3 ÷ 0.25 = £12.00.

What if suppliers increase their prices?

Recalculate portion cost and GP whenever pack prices change. With the full Mise en Price supplier price book, confirmed changes to linked ingredient prices can feed into saved recipe costings.

Can I use this for catering and batch cooking?

Yes. Calculate ingredient costs for the whole batch, divide by portions or covers, then compare each portion against its excluding-VAT selling price. Also budget separately for labour, delivery, equipment, overheads and wastage.
