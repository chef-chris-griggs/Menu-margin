A worked kitchen costing

How to cost a 20-portion soup, including bread and butter

Start with the whole dish you serve. Tomatoes and stock are only part of the costing: include oil, seasoning, bread and butter before you divide the batch across your portions.

Illustrative example. These are sample pack prices, not current supplier quotes, a tested production recipe or a customer result. The assumed yield is 20 saleable portions. Check your own cooked yield and enter your own supplier prices on a consistent VAT basis.

1. Calculate the cost of each ingredient used

Divide the quantity used by the purchased pack quantity, then multiply by the whole-pack price. Keep the units compatible. For the tomatoes: 4 kg ÷ 5 kg × £12.50 = £10.00.

2. Divide by the portions you actually serve

At the assumed yield of 20 portions, £24.00 ÷ 20 = £1.20 ingredient cost per portion. If the same batch only produces 18 portions, the unrounded ingredient cost becomes £1.3333 per portion. Changing the yield changes the costing even when pack prices stay the same.

Food-cost GP % = (£4.00 − £1.20) ÷ £4.00 × 100 = 70%. Use ingredient costs and sales prices on a comparable VAT basis. This is not net profit: labour, energy, rent, waste and other operating costs still need to be covered.

4. Re-cost the dish when a supplier price changes

If the 5 kg tomato pack rises from £12.50 to £15.00, the 4 kg used now costs £12.00. That adds £2.00 to the batch.

New batch cost: £26.00. Across 20 portions, ingredient cost rises to £1.30. Keeping the ex-VAT selling price at £4.00 reduces food-cost GP from 70% to 67.5%, a fall of 2.5 percentage points.

To retain a 70% ingredient-based GP: £1.30 ÷ (1 − 0.70) = £4.3333… excluding VAT. Round the selling price up to at least £4.34 excluding VAT to meet the target. £4.33 falls slightly short before rounding the displayed GP. This is a calculation example, not a recommended menu price.

How to calculate food cost percentage and target GP

Food cost percentage is the ingredient cost per portion divided by the selling price per portion, multiplied by 100. Use comparable VAT bases when entering your figures. For example, a dish with £3.00 in ingredient costs and a £10.00 selling price excluding VAT has a 30% food cost and a 70% ingredient-based GP.

Target GP pricing works backwards: divide ingredient cost per portion by (1 − target GP percentage ÷ 100). At £3.00 ingredient cost and 70% target GP, the required selling price is £10.00 excluding VAT. This figure covers ingredients only; allow separately for waste, labour and overheads.

Recipe costing questions

Is the recipe calculator free?

Yes. The one-recipe calculator on our homepage can be used without signing in or entering payment details.

Can I calculate the food cost percentage?

Yes. Food cost percentage is the ingredient cost per portion divided by the selling price, both on a consistent VAT basis, multiplied by 100. Ingredient-based GP % is 100 minus that food cost percentage.

What happens when supplier prices change?

Re-entering revised pack prices shows how the cost of a dish changes. The full Mise en Price app also provides a supplier price book and linked recipe pricing, which can make repeated re-costing easier.
