HOW TO USE IT

Cost a recipe in four steps

  1. Open the downloaded CSV. Import it into Excel or Google Sheets. It includes a worked example for a ten-portion soup.
  2. Enter quantities and pack prices. Keep units consistent for each ingredient. For example, if a pack is 3,000 g, enter the recipe quantity in grams too.
  3. Extend the ingredient formulas. Copy the ingredient-cost formula in column F down if adding ingredient rows. Adjust the ingredient sum range in H2 to include all of your ingredient rows.
  4. Set portions and selling price. H2 calculates ingredient cost per portion. J2 calculates ingredient-based GP as a decimal; format that cell as a percentage in your spreadsheet.

RECIPE COSTING EXAMPLE

What does cost per portion mean?

Ingredient cost per portion is the total ingredient cost of a batch divided by the number of portions produced. For example, a £24 batch making 20 portions costs £1.20 in ingredients per portion.

Ingredient GP % = (selling price excluding VAT − ingredient cost per portion) ÷ selling price excluding VAT × 100. Read our food GP calculation guide for more examples.

This is an ingredient-only calculation; it excludes staffing, rent, wastage, delivery and other business costs. Use consistent VAT treatment for buying costs and sales prices.

When is a spreadsheet enough?

Frequently asked questions

Is the template really free?

Yes. You can download and edit the CSV without an account or payment.

Does it work with Microsoft Excel?

Yes, Excel can open CSV files. Depending on regional spreadsheet settings, formulas or decimal separators may need adjustment. Save a copy in your preferred spreadsheet format after importing.

Can I cost catering batches?

Yes. Change the portion count to your expected yield or guest count and include all of the ingredients. Add labour and service costs separately when preparing a full catering quote.

Can supplier invoices update the sheet?

Not automatically. The template is a standalone CSV. The full Mise en Price app offers invoice recognition and a supplier price book, with review and confirmation required before saving prices.
