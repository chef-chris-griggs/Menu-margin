A practical costing check

What does each portion really cost?

Start with a recipe you already make. For every ingredient, record the quantity used and the price of the purchased pack in matching units. Add those ingredient costs to calculate your batch total, then divide by the number of saleable portions. Check that your yield is realistic: a twenty-portion batch only costs what you expect if it actually serves twenty portions.

For example, a soup batch with £18 in ingredient costs and ten portions has an ingredient cost of £1.80 per portion. At a £6 selling price excluding VAT, that is £4.20 ingredient-based gross profit per portion, or 70% food GP. It is not net profit: staffing, utilities, overheads and waste still matter.

Questions chefs ask

Choosing recipe costing software for your kitchen

How do I calculate food cost per portion?

For each ingredient, multiply the quantity used by its cost per matching unit, add the ingredient costs for the whole batch, then divide by the number of saleable portions. For example, £30 of ingredients across 20 portions is £1.50 per portion. Allow separately for wastage and any costs that the ingredient-only calculation does not include. Use the free calculator to try your own recipe before signing up.

How do I work out a menu price for a target GP?

Divide your ingredient cost per portion by one minus your target food-cost GP expressed as a decimal. At £3 ingredient cost and a 70% target GP, the example price is £10 excluding VAT. This covers ingredient-based GP only, not labour, energy, rent or other overheads. See the restaurant menu pricing guide for a fuller explanation.

What happens when my supplier changes a pack price?

Update the supplier price book and confirm the change. Recipes linked to that product then use the updated ingredient price, so you can reassess your margins. For invoice imports, review extracted names, pack sizes and costs before saving. Read the supplier price tracking guide to understand the workflow.

Is recipe costing software better than a spreadsheet?

A spreadsheet is useful for a small number of dishes and occasional price changes. Dedicated software can help when you manage many recipes, multiple suppliers or recurring invoices.
