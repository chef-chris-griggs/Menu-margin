How to calculate the ingredient cost of a food truck dish

Record every ingredient in one saleable serving, including cooking oil, sauces, cheese, toppings and garnish. For each ingredient, calculate quantity used ÷ purchased pack quantity × whole-pack price. Add the ingredient costs. Check realistic yield and include preparation losses before choosing the number of servings.

Worked example: loaded fries

This example uses illustrative ingredient prices, not live supplier quotations. All prices are compared on a consistent VAT basis.

At an illustrative £6.00 selling price excluding VAT, ingredient-based gross profit is £6.00 − £1.16 = £4.84 per serving. Food-cost GP percentage is (£4.84 ÷ £6.00) × 100 = 80.67%. This is ingredient GP, not overall business profit.

Costs a food truck operator must check separately

The worked example excludes packaging, napkins, cutlery, card processing, fuel, pitch fees, equipment, staffing, utilities, spoilage and other operating costs. Add these to your business pricing model separately. Consider the VAT treatment of your sales and purchases rather than comparing VAT-inclusive and excluding-VAT figures.

How to price a dish for a target food GP

For ingredient cost of £1.16 and a target ingredient GP of 70%, the required selling price before VAT is £1.16 ÷ (1 − 0.70) = £3.87 when rounded up to the nearest penny. That calculation alone does not establish a viable retail price. Check all operating costs, customer demand and the applicable VAT rules.

What happens when suppliers increase prices?

If your fries pack increases from £5.00 to £6.00 for 2.5 kg, the 250 g portion increases from £0.50 to £0.60. The loaded fries ingredient cost increases from £1.16 to £1.26. At the same £6.00 excluding-VAT price, ingredient GP falls from 80.67% to 79%. Small pack changes matter when repeated across service.
