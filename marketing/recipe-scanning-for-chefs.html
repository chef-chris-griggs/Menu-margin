How recipe scanning works

Bring an existing recipe into your costing.

1. Add your supplier prices

Record the products, pack sizes, units and whole-pack prices you buy. You can enter them manually or use invoice scanning and review the results. The recipe scanner uses your price book for product suggestions; it does not obtain live supplier quotes.

2. Read a recipe photo or PDF

Sign in, choose Scan a recipe on the costing screen and select your document. Start with clear, straight, printed text. Check each extracted row against the original, including decimal points and units. Use a clear printed recipe saved as JPEG, PNG or PDF to get started. Handwriting may need further corrections.

3. Confirm the ingredient matches

A recipe may say “olive oil” while your supplier calls the product “extra virgin olive oil”. Review the suggested product and select the correct pack and supplier. Similar names do not prove that two products are interchangeable. You can correct rows, add missing ingredients, remove unwanted text and enter a manual whole-pack price.

4. Check the costing and save

Set the number of portions and review the ingredient prices. An unpriced ingredient leaves the costing incomplete. Importing opens a recipe draft for costing; it does not save automatically. Once the draft is correct, use Save recipe. For GP, enter a selling price excluding VAT and use ingredient prices on the same VAT basis.

A worked example

Check the pack behind the ingredient.

Suppose your printed recipe uses 100 ml of olive oil. If the product you select costs £18 for a 5 litre pack, that ingredient costs £0.36: £18 ÷ 5,000 ml × 100 ml. Confirm that the selected supplier item and pack size match what you use.

If the complete batch costs £30 and makes 20 portions, ingredient cost is £1.50 per portion. At a £5 selling price excluding VAT, ingredient-based GP is 70%. These are illustrative figures on a consistent VAT basis. Labour, overheads and waste are excluded.

Recipe scanner questions

How is this different from invoice scanning?

Recipe scanning reads the ingredients and quantities you use in a dish. Invoice scanning helps read the products, packs and prices you buy. You review both, then connect the recipe ingredients to the right supplier products for costing.

Can I scan recipes without an account?

The recipe scanner is part of the signed-in app and its 14-day free trial. The public calculator works without an account when you enter ingredients manually.

Will every ingredient match automatically?

No. Suggestions need your review. Choose the correct supplier product or enter a manual price. Check quantities, units and pack sizes before saving, and do not treat an incomplete costing as a complete GP result.

Can I use it on my phone?

Mise en Price runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. Choose a readable recipe photo or PDF from your device. See the recipe costing software overview for the rest of the workflow.
