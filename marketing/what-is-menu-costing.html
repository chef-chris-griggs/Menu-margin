What is menu costing?

Menu costing means calculating what each dish costs to make, then comparing its ingredient cost with its menu selling price. It helps you decide whether a dish meets your target ingredient-based gross profit.

Start with the full recipe cost

List every ingredient in the dish, including oil, sauce, seasoning, garnishes and sides. For each ingredient, divide the purchased pack price by the pack quantity to get a cost per gram, millilitre or item. Multiply that unit cost by the quantity used. Add the individual ingredient costs to calculate the batch cost.

Calculate cost per portion

Divide the batch ingredient cost by the number of saleable portions you actually produce. For example, a batch costing £36 in ingredients that makes 20 portions costs £1.80 per portion. Use the final usable yield, not an optimistic projected number.

Calculate food cost and GP

If a portion costs £1.80 in ingredients and sells for £6 excluding VAT, the food cost percentage is (£1.80 ÷ £6) × 100 = 30%. Ingredient-based gross profit is £6 − £1.80 = £4.20, or 70%. The figures are not net profit and exclude labour, overheads and waste unless you have explicitly included them.

Set a target menu selling price

To work back from a target ingredient-based GP, divide the ingredient cost per portion by (1 − target GP as a decimal). At £3 per portion and a 70% target, the illustrative ex-VAT selling price is £3 ÷ 0.30 = £10. Consider market demand and costs beyond ingredients before setting the final menu price.

Review costs when suppliers change prices

Menu costing is not a one-off job. Update prices when supplier pack costs change, recheck recipe yields and review food GP before keeping or adjusting the menu price. This matters especially for high-volume dishes, buffets and frequently changing menus.
